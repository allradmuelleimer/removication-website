In short

Removication sends nothing. The app holds no permission to access the internet. All data — including the contents of intercepted notifications — stays on your device and is excluded from cloud backup.

What the app processes

Notifications from other apps

For Removication to hide a notification, the operating system has to show it to the app. That is what the notification access permission in the Android settings is for. Without it the app has no function.

The following is processed:

  • the package name of the sending app
  • title, text and sub-text of the notification
  • for chat notifications, the name of the sender
  • the notification category (“channel”) assigned by the sending app
  • the time it arrived

Only what was actually hidden gets stored. Notifications that are let through are processed in memory and immediately forgotten.

Hidden notifications go to the Notification Catcher, a list inside the app. In the free version entries are deleted automatically after 24 hours; with Premium after the retention period you set (30 days by default). You can delete any entry, or the whole list, at any time.

What the app learns about your apps

Removication remembers which notification categories your apps use and how often they send. This serves two purposes: the app picker can sort by actual frequency, and you can target rules at individual categories. Only the label and a count are stored — no content.

What the app cannot see

From Android 15 onwards, the operating system marks notifications containing verification codes and one-time passwords as sensitive. Apps like Removication never receive their content. Banking TANs and login codes are technically unreadable to the app.

Field capture (developer tool)

The app contains an explicitly opt-in feature that writes technical details of incoming notifications to a local file, as a basis for building sender and subject rules. It is off by default, has a size limit, and can be deleted with one tap. The file never leaves the device.

Sharing with third parties

There is none. The app has no network connection, includes no analytics or advertising libraries, and creates no user accounts.

The single exception concerns the purchase of the Premium version, which runs through Google Play Billing. Removication only learns whether a valid purchase exists. Payment processing lies entirely with Google, under Google’s own privacy policy.

The app requests no permission for internet, location, contacts, camera, microphone or storage.

App lock (Premium)

Turning on the app lock means choosing a four-digit code. It is not stored in plain text but salted and hashed, like a password. The code never leaves the device and serves only to protect sets and the Notification Catcher inside the app. It is independent of the device’s screen lock.

Backup and changing devices

The app’s database is excluded from Android cloud backup and from device-to-device transfer. When you move to a new device you have to create your sets and rules again — a deliberate decision: notification content does not belong in a backup you hand over.

Deletion

All data lives in the app’s private storage. It is deleted completely when you uninstall the app or clear its storage in the Android settings.

Your rights

Since no data is transmitted to us, there is nothing here to disclose or delete. Access, rectification and erasure under the GDPR are exercised directly on your device. For any questions, reach us at the address above.

Changes

Material changes will be announced in the app and in the Play Store listing. This page carries the date of the current version at the top.

This website

This site sets no cookies, loads no fonts or scripts from third-party servers and measures nothing — which is also why there is no cookie banner. When a page is requested, the host GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc.) processes technically necessary connection data (IP address, timestamp, file requested) in order to deliver the page and defend against attacks. The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR.
